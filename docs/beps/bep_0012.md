# BEP 12: Multitracker metadata extension - announce notes

Status: planned for v0.1 (public torrents; private torrents use one tracker,
see BEP 27 notes).
Source: https://www.bittorrent.org/beps/bep_0012.html
Reviewed: 2026-11-25

This is a working summary in our own words, not a copy of the BEP. Each
statement is labelled "BEP says" (checked against the BEP 12 text) or
"Our decision" (an implementation choice that the BEP does not require).

## Metadata

- BEP says: `announce-list` lives in the top-level metadata dictionary, not
  inside `info`, so it does not change the info-hash.
- BEP says: the value is a list of lists of URLs; the inner lists are tiers of
  trackers.
- BEP says: a client that understands multitracker metadata ignores the
  `announce` key when `announce-list` is present and uses only the URLs in
  `announce-list`.
- Our decision: `MetaInfo.announce_list` is already parsed as
  `Vec<Vec<String>>`; the tracker module re-validates it because the field is
  public and a caller can construct a `MetaInfo` without parsing.
- Our decision: `announce-list` is used when it contains at least one URL that
  passes `TrackerUrl` validation, even if other entries are unusable. If it
  contains no usable URL, the single `announce` URL is used as a
  one-tracker, one-tier list if it passes the same validation; otherwise the
  torrent has no usable tracker. This is a deliberate deviation from BEP 12's
  "ignore `announce` when `announce-list` is present" rule: the BEP assumes a
  well-formed list, and the fallback keeps a torrent with a damaged list
  usable. `announce` is never merged into a usable `announce-list`.
- Our decision: URL parsing and scheme validation happen in the tracker
  module, not in metainfo. Metainfo only guarantees UTF-8 strings.

## Order of processing

- BEP says: tiers are processed sequentially; every URL in a tier must be
  checked before the client moves on to the next tier.
- BEP says: URLs inside a tier are processed in a randomly chosen order. The
  list is shuffled once when first read, then used in that order.
- BEP says: a tracker that answers successfully is moved to the front of its
  tier.
- Our decision: the resulting order is kept for the rest of the session; we
  do not re-shuffle after a success. BEP 12 does not require a later
  re-shuffle.
- BEP says: the worked examples show three single-tracker tiers acting as a
  backup chain, a single tier of interchangeable trackers, and a mixed case
  where both trackers in tier one are tried before the backup tier.
- Our decision: one shuffle at load, using OS randomness, and the order is
  kept for the whole torrent session. We do not re-shuffle before every
  announce. Tests inject a fixed order.
- Our decision: a tier is attempted sequentially, one tracker at a time. We do
  not announce to all trackers of a tier concurrently. This keeps the
  per-torrent socket count and DNS load predictable.
- Our decision: every tracker in the current tier is attempted, up to the
  per-tier cap, even after one of them succeeds; the peer lists of all
  successes in that tier are merged. This follows the "check all URLs in the
  tier" rule and avoids depending on one tracker.
- Our decision: the announce stops after the first tier that produced at least
  one successful response. Later tiers are backups and are not contacted in
  that round.
- Our decision: after a tier round, the first tracker that succeeded in try
  order is moved to the front of its tier. The relative order of the other
  trackers is unchanged.
- Our decision: a failed tracker stays in place. There is no demotion and no
  removal, so a tracker that was down for one round is retried in the next.
- Our decision: if every tracker in every tier fails, the announce round
  fails as a whole. The state machine backs off and the next round starts at
  tier 0.

## Validation and caps

- Our decision: only `http`, `https` and `udp` URLs are accepted. Other
  schemes are dropped with a counted warning; the raw URL is not logged.
- Our decision: a URL without a host, with a non-numeric or out-of-range port,
  or with userinfo is dropped. See ADR 0003 for the redaction helper and the
  target policy.
- Our decision: duplicate URLs are removed after normalization (scheme and
  host lowercased, default port removed), keeping the first occurrence.
  Comparison is on the normalized URL, not on the raw string.
- Our decision: at most 32 tracker URLs, 8 tiers, and 4 URLs per tier are
  kept. Extra entries are dropped in document order and a counter is
  incremented. The caps bound DNS work, sockets and memory per torrent.
- Our decision: tiers that become empty after validation are removed. If no
  usable URL remains after the `announce` fallback, the torrent has no tracker
  and the session reports `TrackerError::NoUsableTracker` instead of
  announcing to nothing.

## Private torrents

- BEP says (BEP 27): a private torrent must use only one tracker at a time and
  must switch only when that tracker fails.
- Our decision: when `MetaInfo.private` is true, the tier structure is
  flattened into one ordered candidate list and exactly one tracker is active
  at a time. The BEP 12 fan-out and merge rules do not apply. The 32-URL,
  8-tier and 4-per-tier caps are applied at parse time before flattening, so
  the flattened list holds at most 32 URLs. Details are in the BEP 27 notes.
- Our decision: the shuffle still applies once at load, so the first active
  private tracker is chosen at random among the listed URLs. After a failure,
  the next candidate in order is tried.

## Limits

Our decision:

| Limit | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Tracker URLs per torrent | 32 | Bound DNS work and sockets |
| Tiers per torrent | 8 | Bound the sequential search |
| URLs per tier | 4 | Bound one tier round |
| Announce concurrency per tier | 1 | Predictable socket and DNS load |
| Shuffle | once per session | BEP 12 order is stable after first read |

## Tests

- Our decision: unit tests cover a single tier, multiple tiers, an empty
  `announce-list`, a malformed tier entry, duplicate URLs, and the caps.
- Our decision: a deterministic shuffle is injected so the expected try order
  is asserted exactly.
- Our decision: a fake tracker records the order of requests and proves that
  all URLs of tier one are tried before tier two, that a successful tracker
  moves to the front, and that later tiers are skipped when tier one succeeds.
- Our decision: a round after every tracker failed starts at tier 0, even when
  an earlier round succeeded in a later tier.
- Our decision: a private-torrent test proves that only one tracker is
  contacted per round and that no second tracker is used while the first
  answers.
- Our decision: URL validation and dedup are pure functions and are tested
  without a network.

## Known gaps

- Our decision: no scrape, so tier selection is based on announce only.
- Our decision: no per-tracker health score or long-term statistics. A tracker
  is only "last success" or "not".
- Our decision: no concurrent fan-out within a tier, even when a tier holds
  interchangeable trackers.
- Our decision: no tracker URL update from a redirect or from a `tracker id`
  response beyond the session-local `trackerid` value.
